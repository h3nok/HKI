# High-Risk Drug Interaction Guidelines

## Purpose
This reference document catalogs the most clinically significant drug-drug interactions encountered at HKI Pharmacy. Pharmacists must intervene when these combinations are detected during prescription processing.

## Severity Classification
- **Level 1 — Contraindicated**: Must not be dispensed together. Contact prescriber for alternative.
- **Level 2 — Major**: Potential for serious harm. Requires prescriber confirmation and documented clinical rationale.
- **Level 3 — Moderate**: Monitor closely. Counsel patient on warning signs.

---

## Level 1 — Contraindicated Combinations

### Warfarin + Miconazole (oral/topical)
- **Risk**: Severe potentiation of anticoagulant effect; life-threatening bleeding.
- **Mechanism**: Miconazole inhibits CYP2C9, the primary metabolic pathway of warfarin.
- **Action**: Do not dispense. Recommend fluconazole alternative with INR monitoring, or non-azole antifungal.

### MAOIs + SSRIs/SNRIs
- **Risk**: Serotonin syndrome — hyperthermia, rigidity, myoclonus, autonomic instability, potential fatality.
- **Mechanism**: Dual serotonin potentiation via reuptake inhibition + MAO inhibition.
- **Action**: Do not dispense. Requires minimum 14-day washout from MAOI (5 weeks for fluoxetine due to long half-life of norfluoxetine).

### Methotrexate + Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole
- **Risk**: Pancytopenia, bone marrow suppression, potentially fatal.
- **Mechanism**: Both agents inhibit folate metabolism via different enzymes (DHFR and dihydropteroate synthase).
- **Action**: Do not dispense. Contact prescriber for alternative antibiotic.

### Simvastatin (>20mg) + Amiodarone
- **Risk**: Rhabdomyolysis.
- **Mechanism**: Amiodarone inhibits CYP3A4 and OATP1B1, dramatically increasing statin exposure.
- **Action**: Simvastatin dose must not exceed 20mg/day with amiodarone. Suggest pravastatin or rosuvastatin as alternatives.

## Level 2 — Major Interactions

### ACE Inhibitors + Potassium-Sparing Diuretics
- **Risk**: Hyperkalemia — cardiac arrhythmias, cardiac arrest.
- **Mechanism**: Both increase serum potassium through different renal mechanisms.
- **Action**: Requires documented potassium monitoring schedule. Counsel patient on symptoms (muscle weakness, palpitations, nausea).

### Fluoroquinolones + QT-Prolonging Agents
- **Risk**: Torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac death.
- **Combinations**: Levofloxacin/moxifloxacin with ondansetron, amiodarone, sotalol, haloperidol, or methadone.
- **Action**: Contact prescriber. Suggest azithromycin or non-QT-prolonging alternative if clinically appropriate.

### Opioids + Benzodiazepines
- **Risk**: Respiratory depression, overdose, death. FDA black box warning.
- **Action**: If combination is clinically necessary, prescriber must document rationale. Dispense with FDA-required patient counseling guide. Limit quantities when possible. Verify PDMP.

### Clopidogrel + Omeprazole/Esomeprazole
- **Risk**: Reduced antiplatelet efficacy — increased cardiovascular events.
- **Mechanism**: Omeprazole/esomeprazole inhibit CYP2C19, blocking conversion of clopidogrel to its active metabolite.
- **Action**: Recommend pantoprazole or famotidine as alternatives. Document if prescriber elects to continue.

### Lithium + NSAIDs
- **Risk**: Lithium toxicity — tremor, confusion, renal impairment, seizures.
- **Mechanism**: NSAIDs reduce renal lithium clearance by 15-25%.
- **Action**: If NSAID is necessary, reduce lithium dose and monitor levels within 5 days. Acetaminophen is preferred for analgesia.

---

## Level 3 — Moderate Interactions

### Levothyroxine + Calcium/Iron Supplements
- **Risk**: Reduced thyroid hormone absorption by 40-50%.
- **Action**: Separate administration by at least 4 hours. Counsel patient at every fill.

### Metformin + Iodinated Contrast Dye
- **Risk**: Lactic acidosis in patients with renal impairment.
- **Action**: Hold metformin 48 hours before and after contrast procedures. Resume only after eGFR confirmed ≥30.

### Ciprofloxacin + Dairy Products / Antacids
- **Risk**: Reduced antibiotic absorption by 30-50% due to chelation.
- **Action**: Take ciprofloxacin 2 hours before or 6 hours after dairy, calcium, magnesium, aluminum, or iron products.

---

## Pharmacist Responsibilities
1. Review all interaction alerts generated by the pharmacy management system — do not override without clinical justification.
2. Document all Level 1 and Level 2 interventions in the patient's profile.
3. Contact the prescriber for any Level 1 interaction before dispensing.
4. Provide written counseling materials for Level 2 interactions.
5. Report recurring interaction patterns to the Pharmacy Manager for prescriber education outreach.
