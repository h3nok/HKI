# Controlled Substance Dispensing Policy

## Purpose
This policy establishes procedures for the receipt, storage, dispensing, record-keeping, and disposal of controlled substances at HKI Pharmacy in compliance with DEA, state pharmacy board, and federal regulations.

## DEA Scheduling Overview

| Schedule | Description | Examples | Refill Policy |
|----------|-------------|----------|---------------|
| II | High abuse potential, severe dependence | Oxycodone, fentanyl, amphetamine, methylphenidate | No refills. New Rx each time. |
| III | Moderate abuse potential | Testosterone, buprenorphine, ketamine | Up to 5 refills in 6 months |
| IV | Lower abuse potential | Benzodiazepines, zolpidem, tramadol | Up to 5 refills in 6 months |
| V | Lowest abuse potential | Pregabalin, cough syrups with codeine | Up to 5 refills in 6 months |

## Receiving Controlled Substances

### Schedule II
- Must be ordered via DEA Form 222 (triplicate) or CSOS (Controlled Substance Ordering System) electronic equivalent.
- Upon receipt: verify quantity matches order, check for tampered packaging, record lot numbers.
- File DEA Form 222 Copy 3 chronologically. Retain for minimum 2 years.
- Any discrepancy between ordered and received quantity must be reported to the Pharmacy Manager and documented on DEA Form 106 if theft/loss is suspected.

### Schedules III–V
- May be ordered through normal wholesale channels.
- Verify invoice quantities upon receipt. File invoices separately from non-controlled records.

## Storage Requirements
- All controlled substances stored in a substantially constructed, locked cabinet or safe, or dispersed throughout non-controlled inventory (per DEA guidelines).
- HKI Pharmacy standard: Schedule II medications stored in a time-delay safe with dual-access controls (pharmacist + manager PIN).
- Surveillance cameras must cover the controlled substance storage area with minimum 90-day recording retention.
- Key/combination access limited to licensed pharmacists. Log of access maintained.

## Dispensing Procedures

### PDMP (Prescription Drug Monitoring Program) Check
- **Mandatory** before dispensing any Schedule II, III, or IV substance.
- Check the patient's controlled substance history for the past 12 months.
- Red flags requiring pharmacist clinical judgment:
  - Multiple prescribers for the same drug class within 90 days
  - Overlapping opioid and benzodiazepine prescriptions
  - Escalating quantities or early refill patterns
  - Cash payment for controlled substances when patient has insurance
  - Long-distance travel to pharmacy (out-of-area prescriber or patient)
  - Prescriptions from known high-volume prescribers

### Corresponding Responsibility Doctrine
Pharmacists have a **corresponding responsibility** (21 CFR 1306.04) to ensure that a controlled substance prescription is issued for a legitimate medical purpose. A pharmacist must refuse to fill a prescription if, in their professional judgment, the prescription is not legitimate, even if it appears technically valid.

### Partial Fills

**Schedule II**:
- Permitted if pharmacy cannot supply full quantity — must supply remainder within 72 hours (or Rx is voided for the remaining amount).
- For patients in long-term care facilities (LTCF) or with terminal illness: partial fills permitted for up to 60 days from date of issuance.
- For all other patients (per SUPPORT Act, 2018): partial fills permitted at patient or prescriber request for up to 30 days from date of issuance.

**Schedules III–V**:
- Partial fills permitted. Total quantity dispensed across all partial fills must not exceed the total prescribed. All partial fills must be completed within 6 months.

### Early Refill Policy
- Schedule II: Not applicable (no refills).
- Schedules III–V: Generally not before 75% of the days' supply has elapsed (e.g., 30-day supply → refillable on day 23).
- Exceptions requiring pharmacist approval and documentation:
  - Lost/stolen medication (limit: once per 12 months per patient, police report encouraged)
  - Travel supply (documented travel dates)
  - Dosage change by prescriber (new Rx required)
  - Insurance-mandated early fill (e.g., prior authorization change)

## Inventory Control

### Perpetual Inventory
- HKI Pharmacy maintains a perpetual inventory for all Schedule II medications.
- Every transaction (receipt, dispensed, return, waste, transfer) logged in real-time.
- Physical count reconciled with perpetual inventory **weekly** by two staff members (dual-verification).
- Any discrepancy >1 unit triggers immediate investigation.

### Biennial Inventory
- Complete physical inventory of ALL controlled substances (Schedules II–V) conducted every 2 years per DEA requirement.
- Inventory date, time (indicate AM or PM), and names of counters documented.
- Filed separately and retained for minimum 2 years.

### Annual Self-Audit
- Pharmacy Manager conducts comprehensive audit including:
  - Reconciliation of top 20 dispensed controlled substances (by volume)
  - DEA Form 222 reconciliation
  - Reverse distribution documentation
  - PDMP query compliance rate
  - Surveillance system functionality check

## Theft, Loss, and Diversion

### Immediate Actions
1. Secure the area and preserve evidence.
2. Notify Pharmacy Manager and District Pharmacy Supervisor.
3. Contact local law enforcement if theft is suspected.
4. File DEA Form 106 (Report of Theft or Loss) within one business day.
5. Notify the state Board of Pharmacy per state-specific timelines.

### Diversion Indicators
- Unexplained inventory discrepancies
- Unusual patterns in void/return transactions
- Staff behavioral changes (isolation, defensiveness, frequent bathroom breaks)
- Controlled substance prescriptions for staff members filled at the same pharmacy
- Discrepancies between dispensing records and surveillance footage

### Prevention Measures
- Dual-count verification for all Schedule II dispensing
- Randomized locker/bag checks (per employee handbook acknowledgment)
- Annual controlled substance training for all pharmacy staff
- Anonymous reporting hotline

## Disposal and Reverse Distribution
- Expired, damaged, or recalled controlled substances sent to DEA-registered reverse distributor.
- Document: drug name, strength, quantity, lot number, date sent, reverse distributor name and DEA number.
- Maintain proof of destruction/receipt from reverse distributor for 2 years.
- Patient drug take-back: HKI participates in DEA National Prescription Drug Take Back Day and maintains authorized collection receptacles where permitted by state law.

## Emergency Dispensing
- In a bona fide emergency, a pharmacist may dispense a Schedule II substance upon oral authorization from a prescriber.
- The prescriber must provide a written/electronic prescription within 7 days.
- Quantity limited to the amount needed for the emergency period.
- Document: prescriber name, drug, quantity, date, patient name, and reason for emergency.

## Record Retention
| Record Type | Retention Period |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Prescriptions (all schedules) | 2 years minimum (state law may require longer) |
| DEA Form 222 | 2 years |
| DEA Form 106 | 2 years |
| Perpetual inventory logs | 2 years |
| Biennial inventory | 2 years |
| PDMP query logs | Per state requirement |
| Reverse distribution records | 2 years |
