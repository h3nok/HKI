# HKI Pharmacy Immunization Program

## Overview
HKI Pharmacy provides immunization services across all locations with a licensed pharmacist on duty. This document covers protocols, eligible vaccines, patient screening, documentation, and emergency procedures.

## Pre-Vaccination Screening

### Required Screening Questions
1. Are you feeling sick today (fever, acute illness)?
2. Do you have any allergies to vaccines or vaccine components (eggs, gelatin, neomycin, latex)?
3. Have you ever had a serious reaction to a vaccine?
4. Do you have a weakened immune system (HIV, cancer treatment, transplant, chronic steroids)?
5. Are you pregnant or planning to become pregnant in the next 4 weeks?
6. Have you received any other vaccines in the past 4 weeks?
7. Have you ever had Guillain-Barré Syndrome?
8. For COVID vaccine: Have you received monoclonal antibodies or convalescent plasma in the past 90 days?
9. For live vaccines: Are you taking any immunosuppressive medications?

### Contraindications Requiring Referral
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) to a previous dose or vaccine component
- Known severe immunodeficiency for live vaccines (MMR, varicella, live zoster)
- Pregnancy for live vaccines
- Age outside FDA-approved range
- Active moderate-to-severe febrile illness (defer until resolved)

## Administration Protocol

### Preparation
1. Verify vaccine identity: name, lot number, expiration date.
2. Check Vaccine Information Statement (VIS) — provide current VIS to patient.
3. Inspect vaccine vial for discoloration, particulate matter, or compromise.
4. Draw up vaccine using appropriate needle gauge and length:
   - IM deltoid: 22-25 gauge, 1-1.5 inch (adjust for body habitus)
   - Subcut: 25 gauge, 5/8 inch
5. Patient positioning: seated, arm relaxed at side.

### Injection Technique
- Clean injection site with alcohol swab, allow to dry.
- For IM: insert needle at 90° angle, aspirate is NOT required (per CDC/ACIP).
- For Subcut: pinch skin, insert at 45° angle.
- Inject vaccine at a steady rate.
- Withdraw needle, apply gentle pressure with gauze. Do not massage site.
- Activate needle safety device and dispose in sharps container immediately.

### Post-Vaccination
- Monitor patient for minimum **15 minutes** (30 minutes if history of anaphylaxis or syncope).
- Document in state immunization registry (IIS) within 24 hours.
- Provide aftercare instructions: soreness management, when to seek medical attention.

## Anaphylaxis Emergency Protocol

### Recognition
Symptoms within minutes of vaccination: urticaria, angioedema, wheezing, stridor, hypotension, tachycardia, loss of consciousness.

### Immediate Response
1. **Call 911** immediately.
2. Position patient supine (or seated if breathing difficulty).
3. Administer **epinephrine 1:1000 (0.3mg IM)** in the mid-outer thigh.
   - Pediatric dose: 0.15mg for patients <30kg.
4. Repeat epinephrine every 5-15 minutes if no improvement (up to 3 doses).
5. Monitor vital signs continuously.
6. Administer supplemental oxygen if available.
7. Establish IV access if trained and equipment available.
8. Secondary medications (after epinephrine): diphenhydramine 50mg IM/IV, famotidine 20mg IV.
9. Transfer care to EMS upon arrival.

### Required Emergency Supplies (Checked Monthly)
- Epinephrine auto-injectors (adult + pediatric) — minimum 2 each
- Blood pressure cuff and stethoscope
- Oral airways (sizes 0-5)
- Pocket mask or bag-valve mask
- Timer/stopwatch
- Emergency protocol quick-reference card (laminated, posted in immunization area)

## Documentation & Reporting

### Patient Record
- Vaccine name, manufacturer, lot number, expiration date
- Site and route of administration
- VIS edition date and date provided to patient
- Administering pharmacist name and signature
- Any adverse events observed during monitoring period

### State Immunization Registry
- All administered vaccines must be reported to the state IIS within 24 hours.
- Use bidirectional query to check patient's existing immunization history before administering.

### VAERS Reporting
Report to the Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System:
- Any adverse event listed in the VAERS Table of Reportable Events
- Any clinically significant adverse event, even if not certain it was caused by the vaccine
- Filing deadline: within 7 days for events on the reportable table

## Vaccine Storage & Handling

### Temperature Requirements
- **Refrigerated vaccines (2-8°C / 36-46°F)**: Most inactivated vaccines, Shingrix, Heplisav-B
- **Frozen vaccines (-50 to -15°C / -58 to 5°F)**: Varicella, MMRV, some mRNA COVID formulations
- **Ultra-cold (-90 to -60°C)**: Certain mRNA vaccine presentations (pharmacy-specific freezer required)

### Monitoring
- Digital data loggers (DDL) with current, minimum, and maximum temperature display.
- Temperature checked and recorded twice daily (morning and evening).
- Excursion protocol: if temperature is out of range, mark stock "DO NOT USE," contact manufacturer for viability guidance.
- Backup thermometer available for verification.

### Cold Chain Breaks
- Immediately quarantine affected inventory.
- Document: time discovered, temperature reading, duration of excursion (if known), vaccines affected.
- Contact CDC/manufacturer stability data line.
- Do not discard until guidance received — some vaccines tolerate brief excursions.
