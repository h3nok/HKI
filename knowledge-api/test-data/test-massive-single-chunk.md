## 1. Cardiovascular Agents

### 1.1 ACE Inhibitors
Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors block the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, reducing vasoconstriction and aldosterone secretion. First-line therapy for hypertension, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), diabetic nephropathy, and post-MI left ventricular dysfunction.

**Medications:**
- **Lisinopril**: 5-40mg daily. Most prescribed ACE inhibitor. No hepatic metabolism (renally cleared). Monitor: potassium, creatinine, cough. Black box warning: pregnancy (Category D — fetal renal damage, oligohydramnios, skull ossification defects). Common ADRs: dry cough (10-15%), hyperkalemia, angioedema (0.1-0.7%, higher in Black patients), dizziness, headache, fatigue. Drug interactions: potassium-sparing diuretics (hyperkalemia), NSAIDs (reduced efficacy + renal risk), lithium (increased levels), aliskiren (contraindicated in diabetes/renal impairment). Renal dosing: CrCl 10-30: start 2.5mg; CrCl <10 or HD: start 2.5mg. Counseling: avoid salt substitutes (KCl), report swelling of face/lips/tongue immediately, may cause first-dose hypotension — take at bedtime initially.

- **Enalapril**: 5-40mg daily in 1-2 divided doses. Prodrug (hepatic conversion to enalaprilat). IV form available (enalaprilat 1.25mg q6h). Pediatric dosing: 0.08mg/kg/day up to 5mg/day. Monitoring same as lisinopril. Unique consideration: oral solution available for patients who cannot swallow tablets. Storage: protect from moisture.

- **Ramipril**: 2.5-20mg daily in 1-2 divided doses. HOPE trial demonstrated cardiovascular mortality reduction independent of blood pressure lowering. Preferred in high CV risk patients. Hepatically metabolized to ramiprilat (active). Consider in: post-MI, stable CAD, diabetes with ≥1 CV risk factor, peripheral vascular disease, prior stroke.

- **Benazepril**: 10-40mg daily in 1-2 divided doses. Highly protein bound. Dual elimination (hepatic + renal). Combination product: benazepril/amlodipine (Lotrel). Renal dosing: CrCl <30: start 5mg/day.

- **Fosinopril**: 10-40mg daily. Unique among ACEIs: balanced hepatic/renal elimination — no dose adjustment needed in renal impairment. Consider when other ACEIs require dose reduction due to CKD.

- **Quinapril**: 10-80mg daily in 1-2 divided doses. High tissue ACE binding affinity. Contains magnesium stearate — may interact with tetracycline/fluoroquinolone absorption. Take on empty stomach for optimal absorption.

### 1.2 ARBs (Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers)
Block AT1 receptors. Used as ACE inhibitor alternative when cough or angioedema occurs. Do NOT combine with ACE inhibitors (ONTARGET trial: no benefit, increased adverse events).

**Medications:**
- **Losartan**: 25-100mg daily in 1-2 doses. Unique: uricosuric effect (lowers uric acid — beneficial in gout). CYP2C9/3A4 substrate — active metabolite EXP3174 is 10-40x more potent. Drug interactions: fluconazole, rifampin, phenytoin alter levels. Approved for diabetic nephropathy (RENAAL trial), stroke prevention in LVH (LIFE trial). Renal dosing: generally no adjustment but start low in volume depletion.

- **Valsartan**: 80-320mg daily. Entresto combination (sacubitril/valsartan) is guideline-directed for HFrEF — reduces CV death and HF hospitalization vs. enalapril (PARADIGM-HF). If switching from ACEI to Entresto: 36-hour washout required (angioedema risk). Valsartan recall history (2018-2019): NDMA contamination from certain manufacturers — resolved.

- **Irbesartan**: 150-300mg daily. Longest half-life among ARBs (11-15h). IDNT and IRMA-2 trials: renal protection in type 2 diabetic nephropathy. Minimal CYP metabolism — fewer drug interactions. No dose adjustment in renal or hepatic impairment.

- **Olmesartan**: 20-40mg daily. FDA warning (2013): sprue-like enteropathy with chronic use (severe diarrhea, weight loss, villous atrophy). Discontinue if unexplained GI symptoms. Otherwise well-tolerated.

- **Telmisartan**: 20-80mg daily. Longest half-life (24h), highest volume of distribution. Unique PPAR-gamma partial agonist activity — may improve insulin sensitivity. ONTARGET/TRANSCEND trials. Avoid in biliary obstruction (hepatically eliminated).

- **Candesartan**: 8-32mg daily. Highest AT1 receptor binding affinity and slowest dissociation rate. CHARM trials: HF benefit. Off-label: migraine prophylaxis (16mg/day — Level A evidence, comparable to propranolol).

- **Azilsartan (Edarbi)**: 40-80mg daily. Newest ARB. Head-to-head superiority over olmesartan and valsartan for BP reduction at maximum doses. Combination with chlorthalidone (Edarbyclor). Not studied in HF.

### 1.3 Beta-Blockers
Negative chronotropic and inotropic effects. Three phenotypes: beta-1 selective (cardioselective), non-selective (beta-1 + beta-2), and combined alpha/beta blockers.

**Cardioselective (beta-1):**
- **Metoprolol succinate (Toprol-XL)**: 25-200mg daily. Extended release. Guideline-directed for HFrEF (MERIT-HF trial: 34% mortality reduction). Start low in HF (12.5-25mg), uptitrate every 2 weeks. Do not crush or chew ER tablet. Available as Metoprolol tartrate (Lopressor) for acute settings — 25-100mg BID. IV: 5mg q5min x 3 doses for acute rate control. CYP2D6 substrate: poor metabolizers have 5x higher exposure. Drug interactions: fluoxetine, paroxetine, bupropion (CYP2D6 inhibitors) increase levels significantly.

- **Atenolol**: 25-100mg daily. Hydrophilic — does not cross BBB well (fewer CNS side effects). Renally eliminated — dose adjust in CKD. ASCOT-BPLA trial raised concerns about CV outcomes vs. amlodipine — no longer first-line for uncomplicated HTN in many guidelines. Still useful for rate control, migraine prophylaxis, essential tremor.

- **Bisoprolol**: 2.5-10mg daily. Most beta-1 selective. CIBIS-II trial: HFrEF mortality reduction. Less bronchospasm risk than other beta-blockers. Balanced hepatic/renal elimination. First-line in HFrEF guidelines alongside metoprolol succinate and carvedilol.

- **Nebivolol**: 5-40mg daily. Unique: nitric oxide-mediated vasodilation. May be better tolerated (less fatigue, less sexual dysfunction). Beta-1 selectivity dose-dependent (lost at >10mg). More expensive. Limited HF data.

**Non-selective:**
- **Propranolol**: 40-320mg daily (IR: BID-TID; ER: daily). Lipophilic — crosses BBB (anxiolytic, migraine prophylaxis, essential tremor, performance anxiety). Inderal LA (ER). Drug interactions: CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 substrate. Contraindications: asthma (absolute), decompensated HF, cocaine-induced chest pain (unopposed alpha). Unique uses: thyrotoxicosis (decreases T4→T3 conversion), variceal bleeding prophylaxis (portal HTN), infantile hemangioma (Hemangeol).

- **Nadolol**: 40-320mg daily. Longest half-life (14-24h) among beta-blockers. Renally eliminated. Used for variceal bleed prophylaxis, rate control. Does not cross BBB.

- **Timolol**: Primarily ophthalmic (glaucoma). Systemic effects possible from eye drops: bradycardia, bronchospasm, fatigue. Counsel patients to perform nasolacrimal occlusion after instillation.

**Alpha/Beta blockers:**
- **Carvedilol**: 3.125-25mg BID (IR) or 10-80mg daily (CR). Guideline-directed for HFrEF (COPERNICUS, COMET trials). Alpha-1 blockade → additional vasodilation. Antioxidant properties. Take with food (slows absorption, reduces orthostatic hypotension risk). Phosphorous CR formulation can be opened and sprinkled on applesauce.

- **Labetalol**: 200-2400mg daily in BID dosing. IV: 20mg bolus, then 40-80mg q10min (max 300mg) or infusion 0.5-2mg/min. Preferred in pregnancy-related hypertension (preeclampsia). Alpha:beta ratio is 1:3 (oral) and 1:7 (IV). Minimal placental transfer compared to other beta-blockers.

### 1.4 Calcium Channel Blockers
Two subclasses: dihydropyridines (vascular selective) and non-dihydropyridines (cardiac selective).

**Dihydropyridines:**
- **Amlodipine**: 2.5-10mg daily. Long half-life (30-50h). No significant food interaction. ALLHAT trial: superior to lisinopril for stroke prevention in Black patients. Most commonly prescribed CCB globally. Peripheral edema is dose-dependent (up to 10% at 10mg). Combination products: amlodipine/benazepril, amlodipine/atorvastatin (Caduet), amlodipine/valsartan, amlodipine/olmesartan.

- **Nifedipine**: IR (10-30mg TID — avoid for HTN, dangerous reflex tachycardia) vs. ER (30-90mg daily — safe for HTN). Procardia XL: ghost tablet in stool (osmotic pump shell). Tocolytic use in preterm labor. Drug interactions: strong CYP3A4 substrate — avoid grapefruit, clarithromycin, ketoconazole. Gingival hyperplasia (2-10%).

- **Felodipine**: 2.5-10mg daily. Classic grapefruit interaction study drug (6-fold AUC increase with grapefruit juice). Used primarily in Europe.

**Non-dihydropyridines:**
- **Diltiazem**: 120-480mg daily (ER). Rate control for AFib/flutter. Cardizem CD, Tiazac, Dilacor XR (NOT interchangeable). IV: 0.25mg/kg bolus, then 5-15mg/hr infusion. Significant CYP3A4 inhibition: increases levels of cyclosporine, simvastatin, midazolam, apixaban. Contraindicated with beta-blockers in HF (additive negative inotropy).

- **Verapamil**: 80-480mg daily (IR: TID; ER: daily-BID). Strongest negative inotrope among CCBs. Constipation very common (25-40%). IV: 2.5-5mg over 2 min for SVT. Dangerous with beta-blockers (severe bradycardia, AV block, HF). Potent CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibitor: increases digoxin levels 70-100%, increases statin levels. Cluster headache prophylaxis.

### 1.5 Diuretics

**Thiazides:**
- **Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ)**: 12.5-50mg daily. ALLHAT trial first-line. Metabolic effects: hypokalemia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia, hyponatremia. Photosensitivity (increased melanoma risk — FDA 2022 review, no label change). Ineffective when eGFR <30. Duration: 6-12h.

- **Chlorthalidone**: 12.5-25mg daily. 1.5-2x more potent than HCTZ. Longer half-life (40-60h vs. 6-12h). Hyponatremia risk higher due to longer duration. Preferred by some guidelines (NICE, ACC/AHA 2024) based on stronger outcome data.

- **Indapamide**: 1.25-2.5mg daily. Thiazide-like but with direct vasodilatory properties. Less metabolic disruption than HCTZ. HYVET trial: benefit in patients >80 years. Does not require GFR-based dose adjustment.

**Loop diuretics:**
- **Furosemide**: 20-600mg daily (oral) or 20-200mg IV. Most commonly used loop diuretic. Oral bioavailability: 10-100% (highly variable — average 50%). IV:PO conversion: 1:2 (20mg IV ≈ 40mg PO). Peak effect: IV 30min, PO 1-2h. Duration: 6h. Ototoxicity risk with rapid IV push (infuse over 1-2 min per 20mg). Monitoring: potassium, magnesium, sodium, creatinine, uric acid, glucose. Hypoalbuminemia reduces efficacy (diuretic resistance mechanism). Sulfa allergy: low cross-reactivity (<1%) — generally safe.

- **Bumetanide**: 0.5-10mg daily. 40x more potent than furosemide (1mg bumetanide ≈ 40mg furosemide). More consistent oral absorption (80-100%). Preferred in diuretic resistance. Same monitoring as furosemide.

- **Torsemide**: 10-200mg daily. Longest half-life among loops (3.5h). Most consistent oral absorption (80-100%). TRANSFORM-HF trial: no difference vs. furosemide in mortality (2023). Anti-aldosterone properties may reduce cardiac fibrosis. 20mg torsemide ≈ 40mg furosemide ≈ 1mg bumetanide.

**Potassium-sparing:**
- **Spironolactone**: 12.5-400mg daily. Aldosterone antagonist. RALES trial: 30% mortality reduction in severe HFrEF. Anti-androgen effects: gynecomastia (10-15%), menstrual irregularities, sexual dysfunction. Used in: HFrEF, resistant HTN, primary aldosteronism, ascites (cirrhosis), acne/hirsutism (off-label). Drug interactions: ACEIs/ARBs (hyperkalemia), NSAIDs (hyperkalemia + reduced diuretic effect), lithium, digoxin (increases levels 25%). Monitor potassium closely — do not use if K+ >5.0 or eGFR <30 without specialist guidance.

- **Eplerenone**: 25-50mg daily. Selective mineralocorticoid antagonist. EPHESUS trial: post-MI HFrEF benefit. Much less anti-androgen effects vs. spironolactone (preferred in men). More expensive. Strong CYP3A4 substrate — contraindicated with ketoconazole, itraconazole, clarithromycin.

- **Triamterene**: 50-100mg BID. Epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) blocker. Usually combined with HCTZ (Dyazide, Maxzide). Crystalluria risk — maintain adequate hydration. Avoid in severe renal impairment.

- **Amiloride**: 5-20mg daily. ENaC blocker. Lithium-sparing (used adjunctively to prevent lithium-induced nephrogenic DI). Gentler on potassium than spironolactone.

## 2. Endocrine Agents

### 2.1 Diabetes Medications

**Biguanides:**
- **Metformin**: See contradictory dosing test document.

**Sulfonylureas (2nd generation):**
- **Glipizide**: 2.5-40mg daily (IR: BID; XL: daily). Shortest half-life — preferred in elderly. Hepatically metabolized to inactive metabolites. Take 30 min before meals (IR). XL: ghost tablet in stool. Hypoglycemia risk lower than glyburide.

- **Glimepiride**: 1-8mg daily with breakfast. Longest-acting sulfonylurea. Lower hypoglycemia risk than glyburide. Weight gain (2-4kg). CAROLINA trial vs. linagliptin: no CV difference.

- **Glyburide**: 1.25-20mg daily. Highest hypoglycemia risk — AVOID in elderly (Beers criteria). Active metabolites accumulate in renal impairment. Crosses placenta — historically used in gestational diabetes (now less preferred vs. insulin).

**SGLT2 Inhibitors:**
- **Empagliflozin (Jardiance)**: 10-25mg daily. EMPA-REG OUTCOME: 38% reduction in CV death in T2DM with ASCVD. EMPEROR-Reduced/Preserved: HF benefit regardless of diabetes status. FDA approved: T2DM, HFrEF, HFpEF, CKD. Mechanism: blocks glucose reabsorption in proximal tubule → glycosuria (70-80g glucose/day). ADRs: UTI, genital mycotic infections (candidiasis 5-10%), volume depletion, euglycemic DKA (rare but serious — hold before surgery), Fournier's gangrene (FDA warning, extremely rare), lower limb amputation (signal with canagliflozin, not confirmed with empagliflozin). Dose: no adjustment for eGFR ≥20 (for HF/CKD indications). Discontinue for dialysis. A1C reduction: 0.5-0.8%.

- **Dapagliflozin (Farxiga)**: 5-10mg daily. DAPA-HF, DAPA-CKD, DELIVER trials. Similar CV/renal benefits. Also approved for T1DM in Europe (not US). Same class warnings.

- **Canagliflozin (Invokana)**: 100-300mg daily before breakfast. CANVAS program: CV benefit but increased amputation signal. CREDENCE trial: renal benefit in diabetic nephropathy. Monitor feet closely.

**GLP-1 Receptor Agonists:**
- **Semaglutide**: SC (Ozempic 0.25→0.5→1→2mg weekly), Oral (Rybelsus 3→7→14mg daily), Weight management (Wegovy 0.25→0.5→1→1.7→2.4mg weekly). SUSTAIN/PIONEER/STEP/SELECT trials. A1C reduction: 1.0-1.8%. Weight loss: 5-15% body weight. CV benefit: SELECT trial (2023) — 20% MACE reduction even without diabetes. ADRs: nausea (most common, dose-dependent, usually improves in 4-8 weeks), vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, injection site reactions, pancreatitis (rare), thyroid C-cell tumors (rodent signal — boxed warning, no human cases confirmed), gallbladder disease, gastroparesis exacerbation, suicidal ideation (under FDA review 2024). Drug interactions: delays gastric emptying — may affect absorption of other oral meds. Oral semaglutide: take on empty stomach with ≤4oz water, wait 30 min before eating/drinking/other meds. Renal: no adjustment needed. Supply issues: ongoing since 2022.

- **Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound)**: Dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist. 2.5→5→7.5→10→12.5→15mg weekly. SURPASS trials: greatest A1C reduction of any injectable (~2.0-2.5%). SURMOUNT trials: up to 22% body weight reduction. ADR profile similar to semaglutide. Supply constraints ongoing.

- **Liraglutide (Victoza/Saxenda)**: 0.6→1.2→1.8mg daily (diabetes) or up to 3mg daily (weight). LEADER trial: CV benefit. Daily injection (shorter half-life than semaglutide). More nausea than weekly agents (daily dosing = more GI stimulation).

- **Dulaglutide (Trulicity)**: 0.75-4.5mg weekly. REWIND trial: CV benefit. Auto-injector pen (no needle visible — good for needle-averse patients). A1C reduction: 0.8-1.6%.

- **Exenatide (Byetta/Bydureon)**: IR: 5-10mcg BID (Byetta); ER: 2mg weekly (Bydureon BCise). First-in-class GLP-1 RA. EXSCEL trial: neutral for CV. IR must be refrigerated. Injection site nodules with ER (microsphere technology). Avoid if CrCl <30.

### 2.2 Thyroid Agents

- **Levothyroxine (Synthroid, Levoxyl, Tirosint, Unithroid)**: 12.5-300mcg daily. Narrow therapeutic index — brand/generic substitution controversy (FDA: bioequivalent; endocrine societies: maintain consistent formulation). Take on empty stomach, 30-60 min before breakfast (or at bedtime, ≥3h after last meal). Interferences: calcium, iron, PPIs, cholestyramine, soy, fiber, coffee (separate by 4h for calcium/iron, 1h for coffee). TSH monitoring: check 6-8 weeks after any dose change. Goal TSH: generally 0.5-2.5 mIU/L (varies by age, pregnancy, cancer). Pregnancy: increase dose immediately by 25-30% upon confirmation. Myxedema coma: IV levothyroxine 200-400mcg loading dose. Storage: protect from light and moisture.

- **Liothyronine (Cytomel)**: T3 — 5-75mcg daily in divided doses. Rapid onset, short duration. NOT recommended for routine hypothyroidism (fluctuating T3 levels). Use: myxedema coma (with levothyroxine), T3 suppression test, severe hypothyroidism requiring rapid correction. Cardiotoxicity risk.

## 3. Psychiatric Medications

### 3.1 SSRIs
- **Sertraline (Zoloft)**: 25-200mg daily. Most studied SSRI in cardiac patients (SADHART trial). CYP2D6 inhibitor (mild). Diarrhea more common than other SSRIs. Available as oral concentrate (must dilute in 4oz liquid — not grapefruit juice). Preferred in pregnancy/lactation (most safety data). QTc prolongation at high doses. Drug interactions: MAOIs (14-day washout), linezolid, methylene blue, tramadol, triptans (serotonin syndrome risk). Discontinuation syndrome: less severe than paroxetine/venlafaxine due to moderate half-life (26h).

- **Escitalopram (Lexapro)**: 5-20mg daily. S-enantiomer of citalopram. Cleanest CYP inhibition profile. QTc prolongation: dose-dependent (max 20mg, reduced to 10mg in elderly and hepatic impairment per FDA). Most selective SSRI. Efficacy comparable to sertraline with potentially fewer side effects. Generic widely available.

- **Fluoxetine (Prozac)**: 10-80mg daily. Longest half-life (1-6 days; norfluoxetine active metabolite: 4-16 days). Only SSRI with FDA indication for children ≥8 (depression) and ≥7 (OCD). Activating — take in morning. Potent CYP2D6 inhibitor — significant interactions with tamoxifen (avoid), codeine (reduced efficacy), tramadol, many beta-blockers. 5-week washout needed before starting MAOI. Weight-neutral to slight weight loss (unusual for SSRIs). Weekly formulation available (Prozac Weekly 90mg).

- **Paroxetine (Paxil)**: 10-60mg daily. Most anticholinergic SSRI — sedating, weight gain, dry mouth, constipation. Worst discontinuation syndrome (short half-life: 21h, no active metabolites). Potent CYP2D6 inhibitor. Teratogenic: FDA Category D (cardiac defects — avoid in pregnancy). CR formulation reduces GI side effects.

### 3.2 SNRIs
- **Venlafaxine (Effexor XR)**: 37.5-375mg daily. At low doses (≤150mg): essentially SSRI. Norepinephrine reuptake inhibition emerges at higher doses. Dose-dependent hypertension (monitor BP). Discontinuation syndrome is severe — taper over 2-4 weeks minimum. Renal dosing: reduce by 25-50% in CKD. Approved: MDD, GAD, social anxiety, panic disorder. Off-label: neuropathic pain, migraine prophylaxis, hot flashes.

- **Duloxetine (Cymbalta)**: 20-120mg daily. Balanced SNRI from lowest dose. FDA approved: MDD, GAD, diabetic neuropathy, fibromyalgia, chronic musculoskeletal pain, stress urinary incontinence (non-US). Hepatotoxicity: avoid in substantial alcohol use or pre-existing liver disease. CYP2D6 inhibitor (moderate) and CYP1A2 substrate. Drug interactions: avoid with fluvoxamine (1A2 inhibitor). Capsule cannot be crushed — enteric-coated pellets. Renal: avoid if CrCl <30 (AUC doubled).

## 4. Anti-infective Agents

### 4.1 Common Antibiotics

**Penicillins:**
- **Amoxicillin**: 250-1000mg TID or 500-875mg BID. Most prescribed antibiotic globally. Spectrum: Streptococcus, Enterococcus, Listeria, H. pylori, non-resistant Haemophilus. 90% oral bioavailability (food does not affect). Rash: 5-10% (higher with EBV mononucleosis — 70-100%, NOT true allergy). Suspension: reconstitute, refrigerate, discard after 14 days. Resistance: beta-lactamase-producing organisms → use amoxicillin-clavulanate.

- **Amoxicillin-Clavulanate (Augmentin)**: 500/125mg TID or 875/125mg BID. Extended spectrum: adds MSSA, resistant Haemophilus, Moraxella, some anaerobes. Clavulanate dose capped at 375mg/day (hepatotoxicity risk). Diarrhea more common (clavulanate). Take with food to reduce GI upset AND improve clavulanate absorption. ES formulation (600/42.9mg) for high-dose amoxicillin indications (resistant sinusitis, otitis media).

**Fluoroquinolones:**
- **Ciprofloxacin**: 250-750mg BID (oral) or 200-400mg q8-12h (IV). Broadest gram-negative coverage in the class. NOT reliable for Streptococcus (pneumonia — don't use for CAP). Chelation interactions: calcium, magnesium, aluminum, iron, zinc, sucralfate, dairy — separate by 2h before or 6h after. FDA black box warnings (2008-2018): tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, aortic dissection, hypoglycemia. Reserve for infections without alternative. QTc prolongation. Theophylline interaction (increased levels). CYP1A2 inhibitor (inhibits caffeine metabolism — patients may notice caffeine sensitivity).

**Macrolides:**
- **Azithromycin**: 250-500mg daily or Z-pack (500mg day 1, 250mg days 2-5). Unique pharmacokinetics: tissue concentrations far exceed serum (reason for short course). Half-life: 68h (longest of any commonly used antibiotic). Post-antibiotic effect persists 2-3 days after last dose. QTc prolongation (FDA warning 2013). Drug interactions: fewer than erythromycin/clarithromycin (minimal CYP inhibition). Nausea/diarrhea/abdominal pain. Hearing loss (reversible, rare). Resistance increasing globally — may not cover S. pneumoniae in some regions.

## 5. Pain Management

### 5.1 Opioid Analgesics

- **Oxycodone**: Schedule II. 5-30mg q4-6h (IR) or 10-80mg q12h (ER/Oxycontin). Equianalgesic: 20mg PO oxycodone ≈ 30mg PO morphine. CYP3A4 substrate (major) + CYP2D6 substrate (minor). ER formulation: abuse-deterrent (gelling if crushed). NEVER crush/break/chew ER tablets (dose dumping → overdose → death). Common ADRs: constipation (start bowel regimen prophylactically — senna + docusate or PEG), nausea, sedation, pruritus, respiratory depression. Combination products: percocet (oxycodone/APAP — monitor total APAP <3g/day), oxycodone/ibuprofen, oxycodone/naloxone (Targiniq ER).

- **Hydrocodone**: Schedule II (reclassified from III in 2014). 5-10mg q4-6h (only available in combination products: hydrocodone/APAP = Norco, Vicodin). ER: Hysingla ER (20-120mg q24h), Zohydro ER (10-50mg q12h). CYP2D6 converts to hydromorphone (active metabolite). CYP3A4 metabolism. Most prescribed opioid in the US historically.

- **Morphine**: Schedule II. The reference standard for opioid equianalgesia. IR: 15-30mg q4h. ER: MS Contin 15-200mg q12h or Kadian 10-200mg q24h (can open capsule and sprinkle on applesauce — do NOT crush beads). IV/SC: 2-10mg q3-4h. Renal: active metabolite M6G accumulates in CKD → prolonged sedation/respiratory depression. Avoid in eGFR <30. Histamine release (more than other opioids) → itching, hypotension, bronchospasm.

## 6. Respiratory Agents

### 6.1 Inhaled Corticosteroids
- **Fluticasone propionate (Flovent HFA)**: 88-440mcg BID. Most prescribed ICS. Rinse mouth after each use to prevent oral candidiasis and dysphonia. Growth suppression in children: monitor height. Combination: fluticasone/salmeterol (Advair), fluticasone/vilanterol (Breo Ellipta — once daily). Systemic absorption minimal at recommended doses but possible at high doses (adrenal suppression, osteoporosis, cataracts, glaucoma with long-term use).

- **Budesonide**: DPI (Pulmicort Flexhaler 90-180mcg/inh BID) or nebulization (Pulmicort Respules 0.25-1mg BID). Preferred in pregnancy (Category B — most safety data). First-pass hepatic metabolism limits systemic exposure. Combined with formoterol (Symbicort).

### 6.2 Bronchodilators
- **Albuterol (ProAir, Ventolin, Proventil)**: 90mcg/puff, 1-2 puffs q4-6h PRN. Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA). Rescue inhaler for acute bronchospasm. ADRs: tachycardia, tremor, hypokalemia, hyperglycemia. Overuse (>2 days/week) indicates inadequate control — step up maintenance therapy. HFA propellant (CFC-free since 2009). Nebulizer: 2.5mg/3mL q4-8h. Generic albuterol HFA now available. NEW (2024): albuterol/budesonide (Airsupra) — first combination rescue inhaler, anti-inflammatory reliever therapy paradigm.
